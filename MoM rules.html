📋 Minutes of Meeting (MoM)

Introduction

Minutes of Meeting (MoM) are written records that summarize the key discussions, decisions, and action items from a meeting. They serve as an official reference for participants and stakeholders, ensuring that important information is documented and communicated effectively. Meeting minutes help organizations maintain accountability, track progress, and preserve a record of decisions for future reference.

Importance of Minutes of Meeting

Minutes of Meeting play an important role in professional and academic environments because they:

  • Document important discussions and decisions.
  • Provide a record for future reference.
  • Assign responsibilities and deadlines.
  • Improve communication among team members.
  • Help track project progress and follow-up actions.
  • Ensure accountability for assigned tasks.

Rules for Writing Minutes of Meeting

  1. Record Essential Information – Every MoM should include: Meeting title, Date and time, Location or meeting platform, Names of attendees and absentees, Agenda items.
  2. Focus on Key Points – Minutes should summarize important discussions, decisions, and outcomes rather than recording every conversation or statement made during the meeting.
  3. Use Clear and Professional Language – The content should be written in a formal, concise, and easy-to-understand manner. Avoid slang, informal language, and ambiguous statements.
  4. Remain Objective – Minutes should contain factual information only. Personal opinions, assumptions, and emotional comments should not be included.
  5. Document Decisions Clearly – All decisions made during the meeting should be recorded accurately to avoid misunderstandings and provide a reliable reference for future discussions.
  6. Record Action Items – Every task identified during the meeting should include: a clear description of the task, the person responsible for completing it, and the expected completion date.
  7. Maintain Accuracy – Information recorded in the minutes should accurately reflect the discussions and agreements reached during the meeting.
  8. Follow a Consistent Format – A standard template should be used for all meetings to improve readability and maintain uniform documentation across the organization.
  9. Review Before Distribution – The minutes should be reviewed for completeness, grammar, spelling, and accuracy before being shared with participants.
  10. Distribute Promptly – Meeting minutes should be circulated as soon as possible after the meeting so that participants can confirm decisions and begin working on assigned tasks.
  11. Store Minutes Securely – All MoM documents should be saved in a centralized location where they can be accessed for future reference, audits, or project reviews.
  12. Document Informal Meetings When Necessary – Informal meetings such as coffee talks, brainstorming sessions, or networking discussions do not always require formal minutes. However, if important decisions, commitments, or action items arise, a brief meeting summary should be documented.

Do's and Don'ts of MoM

✅ Do's

  • Record facts and decisions.
  • Assign responsibilities clearly.
  • Include deadlines for action items.
  • Keep the content concise and organized.
  • Use professional language.

❌ Don'ts

  • Do not write a word-for-word transcript.
  • Do not include personal opinions.
  • Do not omit action owners or deadlines.
  • Do not use unclear or vague statements.
  • Do not delay the distribution of minutes.

Essential Components of Minutes of Meeting

A well-structured MoM should include the following sections:

  1. Meeting Details – Title, date, time, location or online platform.
  2. Participants – Names of attendees and absentees (if applicable).
  3. Agenda – Topics planned for discussion.
  4. Discussion Summary – Brief overview of key discussions and important points raised.
  5. Decisions Made – Final decisions, approvals, or conclusions reached.
  6. Action Items – Tasks to be completed, assigned person, due date.
  7. Next Meeting Information – Date, time, and location of next meeting (if scheduled).

Mom for Informal Meetings

Formal meetings such as project reviews, client meetings, and management meetings usually require official minutes. However, informal meetings such as coffee talks, brainstorming sessions, or networking discussions may also be documented when important outcomes arise.

If a coffee talk results in: important decisions, project-related discussions, new ideas for implementation, assigned tasks or responsibilities – then a brief meeting summary or simplified MoM should be prepared. This ensures valuable information is not lost.

📄 Example – Standard MoM

Meeting Information

Meeting Title: Software Quality Assurance Weekly Review
Date: 02 June 2026 | Time: 10:00 AM – 11:00 AM | Location: Conference Room B
Chairperson: Project Manager | Minute Taker: SQA Intern

Attendees

Project Manager, SQA Lead, Software Developer, SQA Intern

Agenda

  • Review testing progress
  • Discuss identified defects
  • Plan testing activities for the next sprint

Discussion Summary

  • The SQA team reported that 85% of test cases had been executed.
  • Three high-priority defects were identified during system testing.
  • Developers confirmed that defect fixes would be completed by the end of the week.
  • The team discussed additional regression testing before the next release.

Decisions Made

  • Regression testing will begin after all critical defects are resolved.
  • The release date will remain unchanged if fixes are completed on schedule.

Action Items

Action ItemAssigned ToDue Date
Fix critical defectsSoftware Developer05 June 2026
Execute regression testingSQA Team07 June 2026
Prepare testing reportSQA Intern08 June 2026

Next Meeting

Date: 09 June 2026 | Time: 10:00 AM | Location: Conference Room B

Meeting Outcome

The meeting successfully reviewed the current testing status, assigned responsibilities for defect resolution, and established the plan for regression testing. All participants agreed on the next steps and project timeline.

☕ Example – Informal Coffee Talk MoM

Meeting Title: Coffee Talk – Process Improvement Discussion
Date: 15 May 2026
Participants: SQA Lead, Software Developer, SQA Intern

Key Discussion Points

  • Improving communication between development and testing teams.
  • Reducing delays in defect reporting.
  • Introducing a shared defect-tracking dashboard.

Key Takeaways

  • Team members agreed that a centralized dashboard could improve visibility of testing progress.
  • Further evaluation of available tools is required.

Action Item

Action ItemAssigned ToDue Date
Research dashboard toolsSQA Intern20 May 2026
